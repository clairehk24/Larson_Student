Simulation 1

Time-Out Ankle

Student Simulation Summary

This simulation is used to assess health care providers’ (HCPs) ability to conduct a quick ankle evaluation, make a decision for return to play (RTP), and apply taping for a grade I or II ankle sprain.

Scenario Synopsis

An athlete rolls their ankle during a wrestling competition. The HCP takes the first 90-second injury time-out to evaluate the ankle and determine if the athlete should RTP or forfeit the match. If determined necessary, the HCP may request to extend the injury time-out by an additional 90 seconds or ask for a 4-minute time-out to select and apply tape to return the athlete to the match.

Learning Objectives

  1. Perform an evaluation for an ankle sprain within 90 seconds.
  2. Make an RTP decision with the athlete.
  3. Return the athlete to competition within the required timeframe.
  4. Select and apply a lateral ankle tape procedure (if indicated) by the end of 5 minutes.

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 78

Timeframe for Simulation

One HCP at a time will enter the room, where their patient with an ankle sprain will be on the floor signaling them to come over. HCPs will have a maximum of 5 minutes to evaluate the patient, make a return-to-play decision, and apply tape.

Simulation Situation

It is 11:00 a.m. on the third day of the wrestling championships. Your patient has just rolled their nondominant ankle, and a 90-second time-out has been called. You will need to evaluate the patient within 90 seconds and determine whether the patient should forfeit the match, whether a second 90-second time-out should be requested to determine the patient’s readiness, or whether you need an additional 4-minute time-out to prepare the patient to return to competition.

Setting

Wrestling sideline at a wrestling championship event in Madison, Wisconsin.

HCP Prebrief

Today’s simulation will resemble a live wrestling match injury time-out, where you have a limited amount of time to evaluate your patient and make a return-to-play decision with them. Here are the details for the presenting scenario.

It is the third period of the match, and the score is 10 to 7 with your patient in the lead. This is the high school wrestling semifinal round. If your patient wins, they will proceed to the final to compete as the top wrestler in the country.

When making your return-to-play decision, be specific about why you have chosen to hold the patient out or why they can safely return.

Safety: If any person says, “Time out. Time out,” please stop all actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the standardized patient (SP) needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Rubric: Time-Out Ankle

Criteria Description Score Comments
Injury Assessment Identifies the pertinent chief complaint; uses palpation, range of motion, and special tests.
Determines the need to ask for a 4-minute injury time-out Identifies whether additional time is needed to prepare the athlete to return to play (RTP) or whether the athlete is forfeiting.
Management Correctly evaluates and conducts the next steps for management or treatment—tape and return, stabilize, or remove from competition.
Makes RTP decision with athlete Discusses the RTP decision with the athlete, including the consequences of the injury and RTP.

Scoring levels: 2—Competency meets minimum standards for an entry-level AT; 1—Approaching competence but not ready for unsupervised practice; 0—Inadequate or unsafe.
